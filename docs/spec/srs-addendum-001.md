# SRS Addendum 001 — Identity assurance: universal KYC, avatar identity layer, payments path

Status: **accepted** · 2026-11-25 · Amends PRS-SRS-001 v1.0
Requested by: product owner (munisp) — *"proceed to implement both with safety and privacy first. Every user of the platform is KYC and vetted."*

This addendum records three amendments. The original 306-requirement ledger is unchanged; addendum requirements carry AMD- identifiers and are tracked separately. Where an amendment touches an original requirement, the original text is amended **in place by reference** below.

---

## AMD-KYC — Universal identity verification (KYC)

**AMD-KYC-01 (amends FR-51/SEC-19 gate).** Every account MUST complete identity verification through the designated KYC provider before it is discovery-eligible. Discovery eligibility becomes: attested device (FR-51) **AND** KYC state `verified`. Before this amendment the gate was attestation alone. The gate fails closed: legacy and new accounts start `pending` and are invisible to discovery until verified.

**AMD-KYC-02 (data minimisation).** The platform MUST NOT store identity documents, legal names, dates of birth, or any provider payload. The platform stores exactly: an opaque provider reference, the KYC state, the verification timestamp, and the reviewing actor for manual decisions. Document custody remains with the provider. DSAR erasure (FR-02/FR-04) deletes the provider reference and state; the provider deletion webhook is out of band and organisational.

**AMD-KYC-03 (state machine).** KYC state MUST follow `pending → verified | rejected`, `verified → suspended`, `suspended → verified`, `rejected → pending` (resubmission). Any other transition MUST be rejected by the database, not by endpoint logic — mirroring the FR-29 trigger discipline.

**AMD-KYC-04 (inter-user privacy unchanged).** KYC is platform-side assurance. FR-30 is **not** relaxed: no display name, handle, photo, profile content — and now additionally no KYC attribute — may be disclosed to another user before OPEN. The only inter-user signal is the FR-70 verification badge at the moment of reveal, whose `verified` meaning tightens to "attested AND KYC-verified."

**AMD-KYC-05 (moderation).** KYC suspension MUST be composable with FR-55 suspensions (device key, handle, payment instrument) and MUST be audited in the append-only admin audit log.

## AMD-AVT — Avatar identity layer

**AMD-AVT-01 (amends FR-06).** The profile gains an optional **avatar**: a non-photographic symbol chosen from the curated, pinned registry. An avatar is never derived from the user's photo, never user-uploaded in this release (uploads require a moderation pipeline and a further amendment), and never carries free text.

**AMD-AVT-02 (recognition anchor).** The avatar is the pseudonymous recognition anchor visible **before** OPEN on the match surface and offer — this is what FR-76's convergence surface and the hold-to-wave flow use to keep two concurrent matches distinguishable. FR-87's prohibition is unchanged: no *photo* at the point of match. The avatar is not profile content under FR-30 because it identifies nothing outside the platform; it is a symbol from a shared registry.

**AMD-AVT-03 (stability under consent).** An avatar MUST NOT change while the account has an offer in EXPRESSED, MUTUAL or OPEN, and at most once per 24 hours otherwise — a recognition anchor that shifts mid-negotiation is a confusion and evasion vector.

**AMD-AVT-04 (accessibility by construction).** Every registry entry MUST reference a palette role from the WCAG-audited client palette (NFR-25), so avatar colour contrast is covered by the palette audit rather than re-audited per avatar.

## AMD-PAY — Peer-to-peer payments path (gate revision)

**AMD-PAY-01 (initiates BR-06 revision).** Peer-to-peer transfers are permitted **only** between KYC-verified accounts, through the structured transfer surface (no payment links or amounts in free text — FR-96/SEC-37 screening is unchanged), behind the dual-consent transfer contract delivered in batch 49. This revises BR-06 to admit a sixth revenue line: transfer fees.

**AMD-PAY-02 (TECH-23 unchanged).** Mojaloop and TigerBeetle MUST NOT be deployed until the payments compliance programme is approved. That approval is organisational evidence, not code; batch 49 delivers the in-repo contracts, and the deployment gate stands until the programme exists.

**AMD-PAY-03 (structured transfer object).** A transfer is a structured record and nothing else: an amount in minor units (bigint), a currency, a fee computed by the database, and a state. There is no memo, note, or message field — by construction, not by policy. Free-text payment solicitation stays impossible: FR-96/SEC-37 screening is unchanged and the transfer object gives it nothing to screen.

**AMD-PAY-04 (dual-consent state machine).** A transfer moves only through: `proposed → accepted | declined | cancelled | expired`, `accepted → settled | disputed`, `settled → disputed`, `disputed → resolved`. The recipient alone accepts or declines; the sender alone cancels; either party disputes; moderation alone resolves; the settlement rail alone settles. Legality MUST be enforced by the database (the `record_transfer_event` definer), mirroring the FR-29 trigger discipline — endpoint logic cannot excuse an illegal transition. Every transition lands in the append-only ledger (AMD-PAY-07).

**AMD-PAY-05 (caps and fee).** Safety-first caps are platform constants, not user settings: a per-transfer maximum of 5,000,000 minor units (NGN 50,000.00) and a rolling 24-hour sender cap of 15,000,000 minor units (NGN 150,000.00) over money in motion (proposed, accepted, settled, disputed). The transfer fee is 100 basis points (1.0%), computed by the database at proposal and stored on the row — this is the sixth revenue line admitted by AMD-PAY-01. Loosening any cap requires a further amendment.

**AMD-PAY-06 (eligibility, proved twice).** Both parties MUST be KYC-verified (`kyc_verified_accounts`) and share an OPEN conversation at proposal, with no active `payment_instrument` suspension (FR-55 composability, AMD-KYC-05). Acceptance re-proves KYC for both parties and the OPEN pair — verification at both moments, because a state can move between proposal and acceptance. A proposal expires one hour after creation if not accepted.

**AMD-PAY-07 (append-only ledger).** Every transfer transition MUST be recorded in `transfer_events`, which is append-only for every application path: the application role holds no UPDATE/DELETE grant and a trigger raises on either. Dispute resolutions additionally enter the admin audit log (FR-60). The ledger stores amounts, states and actors — never free text (AMD-PAY-03).

**AMD-PAY-08 (settlement gate made tangible).** The `settled` state is reachable only through the `record_transfer_event` branch gated by the transaction-local `app.transfer_settlement` setting — at batch 49 no component set it and the branch stood reserved for the future settlement rail. AMD-PAY-15 activates the internal wallet rail behind an operational kill switch and AMD-PAY-18 settles accepted transfers through it; the external Mojaloop/TigerBeetle rails remain TECH-23-gated exactly as AMD-PAY-02 requires.

**AMD-PAY-09 (DSAR inclusion).** The FR-62 export document MUST include the subject's transfer history: every transfer where the subject is sender or recipient, with the subject's role, the counterparty, the amount, fee, currency, state and timestamps, and the transfer's event ledger inline. Masking discipline is unchanged — a counterpart's decline still reads as `expired` (FR-32) and blocked pairs stay out of the pair list (FR-34) — but a transfer is a financial record, not a conversation artefact: it is exported even where the pair itself is masked, consistent with the party-scoped transfer list. Transfer rows survive account deletion (FR-03) tied to the anonymous tombstone, exactly like the consent ledger: they are the record of money movement, they contain no free text (AMD-PAY-03), and they identify nobody once the account is tombstoned. Deletion tooling MUST NOT erase or rewrite transfer or transfer_event rows — the AMD-PAY-07 append-only discipline has no erasure exception.

**AMD-PAY-10 (wallet object).** Every KYC-verified user MAY hold exactly **one wallet per currency**; NGN is the launch currency (loosening requires a further amendment). A single platform fee wallet per currency receives the AMD-PAY-05 fee revenue under a pinned, well-known identifier. Wallet creation is self-serve and idempotent for verified users; an unfunded wallet is inert by construction — it records value movements, it cannot originate them. A wallet is a structured record and nothing else: an identifier, a currency, a status (`active | frozen | closed`), and derived amounts. There is no user-facing free-text field — by construction, not by policy.

**AMD-PAY-11 (append-only value ledger).** Every movement of value MUST be recorded in `wallet_entries`, which is append-only for every application path: the application role holds no INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE grant and a trigger raises on UPDATE or DELETE. Each entry carries the running balance, written under the wallet row lock, and the database MUST reject any entry that would leave a wallet negative — solvency is a CHECK, not a convention. Balances are derived (`wallet_balances`): the ledger sum, the pending-hold total, and the spendable remainder. Kinds are signed by name: `fund`, `transfer_in` and `fee_revenue` credit; `withdrawal`, `transfer_out` and `fee_charge` debit.

**AMD-PAY-12 (two-phase holds).** Reservations follow `pending → posted | voided` and nothing else. A hold's terms — wallet, amount, transfer — MUST be immutable once placed; the database enforces the transition table and the immutability, mirroring the FR-29 trigger discipline. A pending hold reduces the spendable balance without moving value; posting consumes the reservation, voiding releases it. Holds are rail-only operations (AMD-PAY-13).

**AMD-PAY-13 (atomic settlement; the AMD-PAY-08 gate extended).** Settlement of an accepted transfer MUST be one atomic operation: the sender's wallet debited amount + fee, the recipient's credited the amount, the platform fee wallet credited the fee, any pending hold naming the transfer posted (it must cover amount + fee exactly), and the transfer moved to `settled` — all in a single transaction, so value and state can never diverge. Settlement is idempotent: a transfer with ledger rows MUST NOT settle twice. Eligibility is re-proved at this third moment: no active `payment_instrument` suspension on either party, all three wallets active. **Every value-moving wallet definer** — funding, withdrawal, hold placement, hold release, settlement — MUST refuse unless the transaction-local `app.transfer_settlement` setting is `on`: the AMD-PAY-08 gate extended over the whole wallet surface. At contract time (batch 53) no component set the gate and no route reached these definers; AMD-PAY-15 activates the internal rail behind an operational kill switch, with this database gate retained as defence in depth. Wallet freezing and unfreezing are moderation operations (AMD-KYC-05 composability), audited in the admin audit log; the platform wallet is untouchable by moderation.

**AMD-PAY-15 (rail activation; the licensing gate becomes an operational kill switch).** The internal NGN ledger rail is live: value moves through the wallet definers of AMD-PAY-11..13, reached exclusively by edge routes that set `app.transfer_settlement` transaction-locally through the edge's settlement helper — the only writer of the setting, so no route can move value outside a gated transaction. A single operational kill switch, `WALLET_RAIL`, refuses every value-moving wire with 503 when set to `off`; the definers' database gate is retained as defence in depth — an unset setting refuses, whatever the caller managed to run. Value enters the system only through an authenticated rail (AMD-PAY-16) or an audited moderation credit. The external Mojaloop/TigerBeetle rails remain gated by TECH-23 exactly as AMD-PAY-02 requires: the compliance programme's approval is organisational evidence, and internal-rail maturity is no substitute for it.

**AMD-PAY-16 (funding).** Value enters a wallet only through an authenticated rail: an HMAC-signed provider callback (`fund_wallet`), or an audited moderation credit. The provider signs a canonical, colon-joined string led by a unix-epoch timestamp (AMD-PAY-22); a callback whose timestamp is missing or outside the freshness window is refused before the signature is even checked. Funding is idempotent by provider reference — a repeated callback returns the recorded outcome and moves nothing twice. A per-event cap of 15,000,000 minor units (NGN 150,000.00) applies. Every funding callback, applied or duplicate, is recorded in `wallet_rail_events`, which is definer-only: no application role reads or writes it directly, and deletion tooling MUST NOT erase or rewrite it (AMD-PAY-20). A manual moderation credit above the four-eyes threshold moves nothing — it opens a pending approval a second moderator must execute (AMD-PAY-23).

**AMD-PAY-17 (withdrawals).** A withdrawal is a two-phase, hold-backed request: the owner requests (reserving the amount as a pending hold), a moderator approves or rejects (audited in the admin audit log), and the payout rail completes or fails the request — the ledger entry posts first, the hold posts after, so value and state can never diverge. States are `requested → processing → paid | failed` and `requested → cancelled`; request terms are immutable once made, requests are append-only, and illegal transitions raise (the FR-29 trigger discipline). Caps mirror AMD-PAY-05: a per-withdrawal maximum of 5,000,000 minor units (NGN 50,000.00) and a rolling 24-hour cap of 15,000,000 minor units (NGN 150,000.00) over requested, processing and paid. The destination is an opaque provider-side payout reference, never raw bank details (the AMD-KYC-03 minimisation discipline applied to payouts). The owner may cancel a requested withdrawal; the hold is voided in the same transaction. Every payout callback, applied or duplicate, is recorded in `wallet_rail_events`, and payout callbacks carry the same signed-timestamp freshness discipline as funding callbacks (AMD-PAY-22).

**AMD-PAY-18 (acceptance settles).** Accepting a transfer is one atomic operation: the sender's eligibility and funds are re-proved (KYC both parties, the OPEN conversation, the sender's wallet active and funded for amount + fee), the reservation is placed, and settlement posts — all in the same transaction, so acceptance and settlement can never diverge. An acceptance the sender cannot fund refuses cleanly and the transfer stays `proposed`. There is no standalone settle route: settlement happens at acceptance through the wallet rail, and it is idempotent exactly as AMD-PAY-13 requires.

**AMD-PAY-19 (reconciliation).** The rail is auditable by construction: a moderator-only reconciliation definer re-derives every wallet's balance from its ledger and reports any mismatch against the stored running balance, the platform fee delta, holds pending longer than 24 hours, open withdrawals, open rail risk signals (AMD-PAY-21), and pending credit approvals (AMD-PAY-23). Reconciliation moves nothing — it reads and reports.

**AMD-PAY-20 (DSAR inclusion of withdrawals).** The FR-62 export document MUST include the subject's withdrawal requests inline per wallet: state, amount, destination reference and timestamps. Withdrawal-request rows survive account deletion (FR-03) tied to the anonymous tombstone, exactly like wallet rows: they are the record of payouts, they contain no free text, and they identify nobody once the account is tombstoned. Deletion tooling MUST NOT erase or rewrite withdrawal_request or wallet_rail_event rows — the AMD-PAY-11 append-only discipline has no erasure exception.

**AMD-PAY-14 (DSAR inclusion of the wallet ledger).** The FR-62 export document MUST include the subject's wallets: per wallet the currency, status and creation timestamp, with the entry ledger (kind, amount, running balance, linked transfer, timestamp) and the hold history inline. Wallet rows survive account deletion (FR-03) tied to the anonymous tombstone, exactly like transfer rows: they are the record of value, they contain no free text (AMD-PAY-10), and they identify nobody once the account is tombstoned. Deletion tooling MUST NOT erase or rewrite wallet, wallet_entry or wallet_hold rows — the AMD-PAY-11 append-only discipline has no erasure exception.

**AMD-PAY-21 (rail risk signals).** Velocity rules run inside the funding and withdrawal definers — where the value actually moves — and raise append-only `rail_risk_signals`: more than five funding events in an hour, more than 30,000,000 minor units funded in a rolling day, or more than three withdrawal requests in an hour, each at most once per rule per wallet while open. Signals FLAG, they never block: value keeps moving and moderation sees everything through a moderator-only review queue; acknowledge and dismiss decisions are audited in the admin audit log. Signal terms are immutable once raised and illegal transitions raise (the FR-29 trigger discipline). Signals are internal risk data — like the admin audit log they are not part of the FR-62 export.

**AMD-PAY-22 (webhook freshness).** Every rail webhook MUST carry a unix-epoch timestamp in `x-wallet-timestamp`, and the timestamp MUST lead the canonical signed string, so a captured signature cannot be replayed beyond the freshness window (300 seconds of clock skew). The edge checks freshness BEFORE the signature; a stale or missing timestamp answers 401. Every webhook verdict — accepted, rejected, unconfigured — is counted in `presence_wallet_webhook_results_total` for the AMD-OPS-01 alerts.

**AMD-PAY-23 (four-eyes moderation credits).** A manual credit above 5,000,000 minor units (NGN 50,000.00) MUST NOT move value on one moderator's word: `fund_wallet` opens a pending `wallet_credit_approvals` row — wallet, amount and reference fixed at proposal time — and answers `approval_required`. A second moderator executes the approval through `approve_wallet_credit`, which refuses self-approval (`self_approval`), re-proves the wallet active, and credits through exactly the provider funding path — same entry kind, same rail-event idempotency on the fixed reference. Any moderator may reject; only execution is four-eyes. Proposals, executions and rejections are audited in the admin audit log. Provider callbacks never take the approval branch — the rail stays live and AMD-PAY-21 watches it.

**AMD-OPS-01 (rail alerting).** The rail's failure modes MUST page: a spike of rejected webhooks (attack or broken credential), any sustained 503 from the rail kill switch, and payout callbacks the definer refuses. The alerts live in `deploy/k8s/prometheusrules.yaml` over `presence_wallet_webhook_results_total` and the edge HTTP metrics.

**AMD-OPS-02 (restore drill).** A backup that has never been restored is not a backup: a scheduled CI workflow MUST boot the database through the real compose wiring — same image, same initdb mounts, so the drill covers the full migration set — take a `pg_dump`, restore it into a second database, and prove the round-trip (identical table count; the pinned platform fee wallet id present). The drill runs weekly and on demand.

**AMD-OPS-03 (rate limits on value routes).** Every value-touching POST route — wallet creation, withdrawal request and cancel, the moderation freeze/credit/decide routes, the governance routes, and the provider webhooks — MUST declare a per-route rate limit keyed by the authenticated identity (the global limiter stays off). A static test asserts the declaration exists; documentation alone is not enforcement.

**AMD-INN-01 (presence bonds).** A user MAY stake a single pinned bond (100,000 minor units) as a showing-up commitment. The bond is reserved through a standard AMD-PAY-12 wallet hold — never a side ledger — and is one-active-per-user by a partial unique index. The owner releases it (the hold voids; nothing moves); only a moderator may forfeit it, and forfeiture MUST name an open report against the bond's owner — the value moves user → platform as `fee_charge` / `fee_revenue` entries and is audited in the admin audit log. Bond terms are immutable and the legal path is `active → released | forfeited` (the FR-29 trigger discipline). Bonds move value only inside the AMD-PAY-15 settlement gate.

**AMD-INN-02 (verified-met receipts).** Two users MAY co-attest that they met: the first submission opens a pinned 900-second window, the reciprocal submission inside the window confirms the receipt. A receipt stores the canonical pair, the two submission timestamps and nothing else — no location, no device data, no free text. At most one pending window per pair; unconfirmed windows expire. Receipts are append-only with set-once timestamps; they feed trust, they never gate it.

**AMD-INN-03 (trust escrow).** Two KYC-verified users MAY structure value as milestones: the payer creates an escrow naming the payee and 1..12 positive milestone amounts summing to at most 5,000,000 minor units; the total is reserved up front through a single AMD-PAY-12 hold. Only the payer releases a milestone (payer `transfer_out`, payee `transfer_in`); when the last milestone releases the hold voids and the escrow completes. Either party may dispute; a disputed escrow resolves only by moderator decision — `refund` voids the hold and refunds the remainder, `release` pays every remaining milestone to the payee — audited either way. Escrow and milestone terms are immutable; the legal paths are `active → completed | disputed` and `disputed → completed | refunded`. Escrow moves value only inside the AMD-PAY-15 settlement gate.

**AMD-INN-04 (group stakes).** A seat at a group table MAY be backed by a pinned stake (50,000 minor units) held from the staker's wallet: at most one active stake per user per table, at most six staked seats per table (the SEC-33 cap), tables open only. The stake is refunded when the edge verifies the FR-100 venue check-in token against the table's venue — presence proven before value returns; the check-in token never touches the database. A moderator sweep forfeits stakes with no check-in refund after a pinned 24 hours: the hold posts and the value moves user → platform as fee entries, audited. Stake terms are immutable; the legal path is `staked → refunded | forfeited`.

**AMD-INN-05 (duress mode).** The client MUST ship a duress exit that works entirely on-device, in a pinned order: withdraw presence (best effort — under coercion the network may be watched or cut), wipe local state through the TR-30 allowlist wipe (complete by construction), and replace the UI with a neutral surface. Every step runs even if an earlier step fails. There is no server route and no duress signal the server can observe: a panic the platform can see is a panic an adversary can see.

**AMD-INN-06 (selective-disclosure grants).** A user MAY issue a single-use grant proving exactly one claim — `over_18` (proven from the declared birth year) or `kyc_verified` (proven from the KYC state) — to a venue verifier. A grant expires 900 seconds after issue, redeems exactly once, and its secret is returned to the holder at issue and stored only as a digest — a database read cannot replay a grant. Redemption answers the claim and nothing else: never the account, the birth year, or the KYC record. Grants are append-only; the single legal mutation is unused → used.

**AMD-INN-07 (on-device match preview).** The client MAY estimate a pair locally from the wire candidate it already holds plus the user's own declared interests: one exact-overlap bonus (0.05, mirroring the TR-04 engine constant) per own interest among the candidate's scored reasons, capped at 1.0, verdicts `strong | possible | weak` at pinned thresholds (0.36 / 0.12, the FR-21 floor). The preview MUST NOT make a request: a server round-trip would turn a private consideration into a platform signal. The server score stays canonical; the preview is labelled an estimate.

**AMD-INN-08 (serendipity windows).** A user MAY declare weekly availability windows — weekday, hour range, market — and the platform surfaces pairs whose windows overlap. At most four active windows per account (a window list is a schedule, and a schedule is identifying). Windows pause and resume; they are never deleted and their terms never change — delete-and-recreate would be a history hole. Overlap answers a boolean between two users, computed server-side: it never reveals the peer's windows.

**AMD-INN-09 (verification passport).** A KYC-verified user MAY mint a portable attestation: an HMAC-signed token carrying a pseudonymous subject (an HMAC of the account — irreversible by the verifier), the proven claim set, and a one-hour expiry. Verification is an unauthenticated oracle — the verifier is a third party with no account — answering `valid` (with the claims) | `expired` | `invalid`, never identity. Every verdict is counted in `presence_attestation_verify_results_total`.

**AMD-INN-10 (host-mediated introductions).** A KYC-verified host MAY propose two KYC-verified parties to each other. A block between the parties in either direction is an absolute refusal. Each party answers for themselves; the first accept is recorded, the second moves the introduction to `accepted` and opens the pair at MUTUAL — written through the FR-29 consent ledger (DETECTED → EXPRESSED → MUTUAL with the parties as actors), never by direct update; OPEN still requires the standard offer flow. Unanswered introductions expire after a pinned 72 hours. Introduction terms are immutable and introductions are never deleted.

## AMD-DATE — Dating mode

**AMD-DATE-01 (opt-in dating mode; FR-87/FR-96 reaffirmed).** A user MAY opt into dating mode by declaring a dating profile: a structured, enumerated attribute set — gender, seeking (one to three genders), relationship goal, and an age-range preference — and nothing else. FR-87's photo prohibition and FR-96/SEC-37's free-text screening are **unchanged**: the schema has no column that could hold a photo or free text, and there is no bio field. Dating mode differentiates on verified identity (AMD-KYC-01) and the avatar layer (AMD-AVT) — a blind, trust-first dating surface — never on appearance. Activation requires `kyc_state = verified` AND an adult declared birth year (SEC-17/BR-03), both proved by the database at write time.

**AMD-DATE-02 (governed vocabularies).** Gender and relationship goal MUST come from pinned, database-seeded vocabularies (`dating_genders`, `dating_goals`) that the application role can read but never write — the AMD-AVT-01 avatar-registry discipline. Changing a vocabulary requires a migration, never an endpoint.

**AMD-DATE-03 (preference-intersection discovery filter).** When the caller's dating profile is active, the scan pipeline MUST intersect matcher candidates with mutual compatibility before any pair is surfaced: both profiles active, both parties still KYC-verified adults (states can move after the write), mutual seeking, mutual age range (computed from birth years server-side), the same goal, and no block in either direction. The filter MUST only narrow discovery — a caller without an active dating profile is unaffected, and the FR-51/AMD-KYC-01 gate still applies first. Dating mode never widens who can be discovered.

**AMD-DATE-04 (dating privacy boundary).** A counterpart's dating attributes MUST NOT be revealed unless the pair is mutually compatible AND stands at NUDGED or later — the FR-30/`pair_avatar` discipline extended to dating attributes, via a single column-narrowed definer. The reveal carries gender, seeking, goal and the age *range* — never a birth year. There is no browse of dating profiles, exactly as there is no browse of users. The FR-29 consent machine is unchanged: dating mode changes who may be surfaced, never how consent walks.

**AMD-DATE-05 (lifecycle and erasure).** A dating profile pauses and resumes; resumption MUST re-prove KYC and age, because both can move while paused. The application role can never delete a profile row (the guard raises); account-deletion tooling erases it as the owner role — a dating profile is pure personal preference data with no counterparty record, so unlike financial records it does not survive erasure. The FR-62 export includes the subject's dating profile.

## AMD-MKT — Marketplace listing primitive

**AMD-MKT-01 (structured listing object).** A user MAY publish a listing: a structured record and nothing else — a kind (`item | service`), a taxonomy category, a price in minor units, the launch currency, a market, and a state. There is no description, no photo, no free-text field — by construction, not by policy (FR-87/FR-96/SEC-37 unchanged). Listing terms are immutable once created: a price change is remove-and-recreate, so a listing can never be repriced under a buyer mid-escrow. The state machine is `draft → listed → held → sold`, `draft | listed → removed`, `held → listed` (refund relists), enforced by the database — the FR-29 trigger discipline. A held listing is never removed out from under an escrow.

**AMD-MKT-02 (append-only events).** Every listing and order transition MUST land in `marketplace_events`, which raises on UPDATE or DELETE for every path — the AMD-PAY-07 ledger discipline applied to commerce.

**AMD-MKT-03 (purchase composition through escrow).** Buying a listing MUST be one atomic operation: a single-milestone AMD-INN-03 escrow (payee = seller, amount = the listing price), the order row naming it, and the listing's move to `held` — created inside the AMD-PAY-15 settlement gate, so value and state can never diverge. The buyer confirms receipt (the escrow milestone releases, the order completes, the listing sells); either party disputes; moderation alone resolves — `refund` pays the buyer back and relists the listing, `release` pays the seller and completes the sale. The order state mirrors its escrow exactly. No new value machinery is introduced: every movement is the batch-56 escrow definers. A verified-met receipt (AMD-INN-02) MAY back the handover as dispute evidence. Listing, order and event rows are financial records: they survive account deletion tied to the anonymous tombstone and deletion tooling MUST NOT erase them (the AMD-PAY-09/AMD-PAY-14 discipline), and the FR-62 export includes the subject's listings and orders.

**AMD-MKT-04 (eligibility and caps).** Seller and buyer MUST be KYC-verified (AMD-KYC-01). The category MUST be a non-sensitive taxonomy node (TR-22 — a sensitive node can never be listed). The market MUST be a configured market (`market_config`). The price MUST NOT exceed 5,000,000 minor units — the AMD-PAY-05 per-transfer ceiling. At most 12 live (`draft | listed | held`) listings per seller: a storefront is bounded because an unbounded catalogue is an aggregation surface. NGN is the launch currency (AMD-PAY-10). Browse MUST project no seller identity (FR-30); the buyer learns the counterparty through their own order row, exactly like an AMD-PAY transfer, and browse reads are limited to KYC-verified callers by RLS.

**AMD-MKT-05 (moderation).** A moderator MUST be able to remove a `draft` or `listed` listing with a governed reason code, audited in the admin audit log. A `held` listing is removed only after its order resolves — the dispute path protects the held value first.

## AMD-ESC — Escrow hardening

**AMD-ESC-01 (four-eyes high-value resolution).** A disputed escrow whose remaining (still-pending) value exceeds 1,000,000 minor units (NGN 10,000.00 — the top band of the AMD-INN-03 5,000,000 minor cap) MUST NOT be resolved by a single moderator. The first moderator's resolution call moves no value: it freezes the escrow, the decision, the remaining value and the proposer into an append-only `escrow_resolve_approvals` row and answers `approval_required`. A different moderator executes exactly the frozen decision in one transaction; self-approval MUST be refused, and a rejection closes the request without moving value while leaving the dispute open. Marketplace dispute resolution rides the same gate because `resolve_purchase` delegates to `resolve_escrow`. Every proposal, approval, rejection and execution lands in the admin audit log — the AMD-PAY-23 credit-approval discipline applied to dispute resolution.
